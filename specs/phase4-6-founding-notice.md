## Why

Six soaks across P4-3/4/5 have produced zero proposals, ever. Two prior findings explain most of it: builders never learned the board's verbs (fixed in P4-5 for self-built boards), and there was never a public grievance to legislate about. This dispatch adds a third variable in isolation: **is an empty board itself the blocker?** Every soak so far started with either no board (wild) or a freshly-built, unused one — no mind has ever seen the propose→vote→close loop actually happen. This dispatch seeds a working example on day one: a notice-board plus one already-open, uncontroversial proposal, so minds encounter a live civic mechanism instead of an inert plank. The orchestrator will watch whether minds (a) vote on the seeded proposal, and separately (b) ever author a proposal of their own afterward — the ask this dispatch answers is (a); (b) remains an open question for the resulting soak.

**Known vote-math constraint, confirmed, not a bug to fix here:** `stepSheepElectorate` votes each sheep agent by `sympathy[proposerId] >= SHEEP_SYMPATHY_YES (0.25) ? yes : no` — sheep never read proposal text. A proposal from a proposer nobody has sympathy for (id `'anonymous'`, no agent, sympathy always 0) gets a reflexive **no** from every one of the ~18 sheep in a 24-agent wild world, regardless of content. The 6 LLM minds are the only agents capable of reading the proposal and voting on its merit; a unanimous 6-yes mind vote still loses 6-18 on raw count. This is by design — sheep vote on trust, not comprehension — and the orchestrator has decided to let it play out honestly: do not touch `stepSheepElectorate`, `PROPOSAL_QUORUM`, or the pass/fail math in this dispatch. The point of this feature is the mind reasoning trace and the *votes cast*, not the tally outcome.
